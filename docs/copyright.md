# Copyright Reality

The uncomfortable truths about copyright, AI art, and what NFT ownership actually means.

---

## AI Art Cannot Be Copyrighted

In the US (and increasingly elsewhere), copyright requires human authorship. The US Copyright Office has explicitly ruled that images generated by AI are not copyrightable.

| Scenario | Copyrightable? |
|----------|----------------|
| Human draws artwork | Yes |
| Human writes prompt, AI generates image | No |
| Human significantly modifies AI output | Maybe (only the human modifications) |

**What this means for NFTmarket:**

When someone mints AI-generated art, there is no copyright to transfer. The "Transfer" license type is selling something that doesn't legally exist. The buyer receives:

- The NFT (a token on the blockchain)
- The watermarked image
- A signed agreement referencing non-existent rights

This isn't fraud - it's the reality of current copyright law. Both parties can agree to treat the work as if it were copyrighted, but courts may not enforce it.

## Legal Recourse for Theft

### AI Art (No Copyright)

If someone steals your AI-generated NFT artwork:

| Action | Likely Outcome |
|--------|----------------|
| Copyright lawsuit | Dismissed - no copyright exists |
| DMCA takedown | May work on platforms, but legally weak |
| Contract claim against buyer | Only works if they signed the license agreement |
| Sue for fraud | Requires proving damages, intent |

**Reality:** Minimal legal protection. Your recourse is social (public shaming, marketplace bans) not legal.

### Human-Created Art (Copyright Exists)

If someone steals your human-created watermarked artwork:

| Action | Likely Outcome |
|--------|----------------|
| Copyright lawsuit | Possible, but expensive and slow |
| DMCA takedown | Effective for platform removal |
| Contract claim | Enforceable if they signed |

**Reality:** Better position, but litigation is expensive. Most individual artists can't afford to sue. The watermark provides evidence of ownership, but evidence isn't enforcement.

## Physical Art Sales

When a physical artist sells a painting:

| What Transfers | What Doesn't Transfer |
|----------------|----------------------|
| The physical canvas | Copyright |
| Right to display it | Right to reproduce |
| Right to resell it | Right to create derivatives |
| | Right to digitize |

**The buyer owns an object, not the image.**

The buyer cannot legally:
- Scan the painting and sell prints
- Use it in commercial materials
- Mint an NFT of it
- License it to others

The artist retains all reproduction rights unless explicitly transferred in writing.

---

## The Opportunity for Physical Artists

A painter could:

1. **Paint the artwork** - copyright exists (human creation)
2. **Photograph it** - high quality digital capture
3. **Mint a watermarked NFT** - with "Transfer" license
4. **Sell both together** - physical painting + NFT

The buyer now receives:

- The physical painting (traditional sale)
- The NFT proving digital rights ownership
- Watermark protection against digital theft
- Signed bilateral agreement on-chain

**Or sell them separately:**

- Sell the painting to a collector (they get the object)
- Keep the NFT (retain digital/reproduction rights)
- Or sell the NFT to someone else (they get digital rights, not the physical)

This creates a legitimate market for reproduction rights that currently doesn't exist in the traditional art world.

## What the Watermark Actually Provides

The watermark is **evidence**, not **enforcement**.

| It Proves | It Doesn't Provide |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Which token an image came from | Automatic takedowns |
| Who minted first (timestamp) | Legal injunctions |
| Chain of custody | Damages recovery |
| That you own the NFT | Copyright (for AI art) |

The watermark helps you prove your case. You still have to fight the case yourself.

---

## Honest Summary

| Art Type | Copyright | Legal Protection | Practical Protection |
|----------|-----------|------------------|---------------------|
| AI-generated | None | Minimal | Watermark + timestamp + social pressure |
| Human-created | Yes | Exists but expensive | Watermark + timestamp + copyright law |
| Physical painting | Yes (artist keeps it) | Strong for artist | Buyer has no digital rights unless NFT purchased |

**For buyers:** You're buying a token and a signed agreement. For AI art, the legal enforceability of that agreement is untested. For human art, you have stronger standing.

**For sellers:** The watermark proves provenance. It doesn't stop theft - it just makes theft provable. Enforcement is on you.
